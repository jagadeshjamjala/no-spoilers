# Company research

The goal is realistic context, never invented facts.

## What to look up (with web tools)

1. **Data stack:** engineering blog posts, conference talks, job posts, and open-source repos that mention their warehouse, compute, streaming and orchestration tools.
2. **Domain and scale:** products, users or transactions, public numbers (annual reports, blog posts).
3. **Recent engineering posts** (last 2 years): migrations, incidents, platform launches. These make great scenario material.
4. **Interview format:** the company's own careers or interview-prep pages first; candidate reports (e.g. Glassdoor, Blind) only as weak signals.
5. **Values or leadership principles**, if published. They shape behavioral questions.

## How to cite and label

- **[JD]:** stated in the job description the user gave. Quote it briefly.
- **[Public, <source>]:** found via search this session. Give the page title and URL. Use only URLs you actually retrieved.
- **[Public, candidate reports]:** interview-format claims from reviews. Always add "anecdotal; formats vary by team."
- **[Assumption]:** your inference from the domain, size or JD. Say what it's based on, e.g. "[Assumption] uses Kafka or similar, given real-time fraud scoring in the JD."

## Rules

- Things you "know" about a company from training data, with no source retrieved this session, are **[Assumption] (from general knowledge, may be outdated)**, never [Public].
- Never write "Company X always asks Y" or "X's loop is 5 rounds" without a source. Prefer "Candidate reports suggest…" with a citation, or "Typical for companies this size: … [Assumption]".
- No web access: state it once ("I can't browse, so company context comes from the JD and labeled assumptions only") and move on. Don't apologize repeatedly.
- Don't pad the calibration summary with research. Use it to shape the questions.
- If the sources conflict, say so and pick the more recent or authoritative one.
